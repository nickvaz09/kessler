From raw orbits to a clear answer.

  1. 1

    Ingest

    Pulls public element sets from CelesTrak and Space-Track, plus conjunction messages when Space-Track has them.

  2. 2

    Screen

    Propagates every orbit with SGP4 across the next 72 hours and finds each pass closer than 15 km. When a solar storm swells the upper atmosphere, it widens each prediction by how far the extra drag can push it.

  3. 3

    Score

    Estimates collision probability, then VIGIL, a model trained on ESA conjunction data, predicts where the risk is heading.

  4. 4

    Explain

    Kessler AI turns the numbers into a short brief in plain language, and names the model behind every risk claim.

  5. 5

    Act

    Replay the encounter in the simulator, try a what-if burn, and keep your satellites on watch.

A model that has to beat the obvious answer.

The hardest baseline in conjunction forecasting is simply assuming the latest risk stays the same. VIGIL only ships if it beats that carry-forward baseline on held-out events, and every prediction records the model version that made it.

    Never a false all-clear.

    A safety tool that guesses is worse than none. These rules hold on every screen.

    No invented numbers

    Every figure on screen comes from the backend or a documented constant.

    Failures are loud

    If a catalog refresh fails or data is missing, Kessler says so instead of showing an empty, calm board.

    Every risk claim is traceable

    Predictions are stored with the model version that produced them.

    Estimates say so

    Screening probability assumes TLE-class uncertainty and is labeled that way. A solar storm widens that uncertainty and never lowers a risk level. Operator covariance is used when Space-Track provides it.

    Demo data is flagged

    Synthetic message sequences used for trend charts are stored separately and marked in the interface.

    Your satellites stay yours

    Objects you add live in your fleet and are screened on demand. They never enter the public catalog or its event tables.

    Kessler

    Collision-risk monitoring for satellites. Named after the Kessler syndrome, the cascade of collisions in low Earth orbit it exists to help prevent.

    Data

    • CelesTrak — public element sets
    • Space-Track.org — catalog and conjunction messages
    • ESA Kelvins — collision avoidance challenge dataset
    • Natural Earth coastlines · NASA Blue Marble and Black Marble imagery
